## Showcase

**CUT! puts a face to the voice: join a fictional story, then direct a character's line and delivery.**

## Write-up (draft, under 200 words)

Writers and narrative designers need a quick way to hear dialogue and explore a different performance. CUT! is a cinematic story player with three authored chapters—Lighthouse, Tavern, and Observatory—and a cast of Odile, Rocco, and Pim. In Director Cut, edit the current line, select one of six emoji moods, then compare a new take with the original. The chapter's authored baseline remains intact. In the story activity, one user idea starts three contextual cast turns, with an explicit option to continue for up to three more. The text reply is rendered as speech and facial video. Nine short Avatar idle clips add subtle motion during reply preparation; they use silent audio, and exact gestures are not controllable. A still illustration represents the group while the user speaks. The lantern is an SVG inspired by the supplied brass hurricane-lantern image. CUT! is designed first for interactive table reads. Language practice, classroom collaboration, and rehearsal are future applications, not finished screens. Native Realtime function tools are not working. Automated checks and one real typed three-turn Chrome beat passed; spoken rehearsal and reaction-cutaway review remain pending.

## How we built it (draft, under 200 words)

The React studio combines three authored scenes, cached character performances, line direction and participant-led conversation. Direct lets writers edit a held line and choose an emoji mood before preparing a Higgs TTS and Avatar take. Original and directed recordings remain available for comparison; the chapter baseline stays saved.

A spoken or typed contribution starts three cast turns. Each completed playback triggers the next speaker, using recent dialogue, the chapter objective, stakes and participant role. Control then returns to the user; Continue story explicitly starts another beat. Cancellation rejects stale responses and stops pending continuation.

Nine Avatar clips generated from silent audio provide subtle idle reactions. Pim blinking and Odile’s head/gaze movement were observed; exact gestures are not controllable. The group listening view is a labeled illustration. Character motion follows system preferences initially and can be changed in settings. Hidden tabs pause idle decoding.
